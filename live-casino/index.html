Live dealer is the one part of an online casino where the outcome is not produced by a random number generator. A physical dealer runs a physical game in a studio, cameras capture it, optical recognition reads the cards or the wheel, and your bets are settled against what actually happened. The result is genuinely physical. Everything around it — the timing, the limits, the rules of that table — is a product decision, and the product decisions are where the money is.

How does live casino work? Live dealer casino NZ explained

Most live tables are run by a handful of specialist studios — Evolution being the largest — rather than by the casino whose logo is on the page. The same table is frequently streamed into dozens of operators simultaneously, which has two practical consequences: the game quality is largely independent of which site you use, and the rules of that table are set by the studio, not the operator. Two sites offering “live blackjack” may be showing you the same table, or two tables whose house edges differ by a factor of three.

A round is on a timer. Betting opens, a countdown runs, betting closes, the hand or spin plays out. The pace is set by the studio and is the main reason live games generate less turnover per hour than pokies — which, since cost is charged on turnover, is a point in their favour.

Live roulette, blackjack and baccarat: the house edge

These follow from the rules arithmetically. Single-zero roulette pays 35:1 against 37 pockets, so the edge is 1 ÷ 37 = 2.70%. Add a second zero and the same 35:1 runs against 38 pockets: 2 ÷ 38 = 5.26%. One extra pocket, nearly double the cost, identical-looking game.

Live roulette NZ: which wheel you sit at

The single most valuable decision at a live casino, and it is made before you place a chip. European and French wheels carry one zero; American wheels carry two. The payouts are identical, so the extra pocket is pure cost: 2.70% against 5.26%.

French roulette does better still where la partage or en prison is offered. Both return or hold half your even-money stake when zero lands, which halves the edge on those bets to about 1.35% — cheaper than any pokie in the building and cheaper than most table games.

Lightning and multiplier variants are the trap. The advertised multipliers on straight numbers are funded by reducing the base payout, so the game becomes higher variance and higher edge simultaneously. It is a genuinely exciting product and it costs roughly three to four times what the standard wheel costs.

Live blackjack NZ: the rules that matter more than your play

Blackjack has the lowest house edge available to you, and it is conditional on rules you can read before sitting down.

The 3:2 versus 6:5 payout on a natural is worth about 1.4% on its own — more than every other rule variation combined, and more than the difference between perfect basic strategy and moderately good play. A 6:5 table turns the cheapest game in the casino into a mid-range one, and it is advertised identically to a 3:2 table.

Two side rules worth knowing. Insurance is a separate bet at roughly a 7% edge, offered at the moment you are most inclined to accept it; declining it is correct essentially always. Surrender, where offered, slightly improves your position and is rarely available online.

Variants also change the maths: infinite or unlimited blackjack seats any number of players on one dealer hand, which removes the wait and multiplies how many hands an hour you play — and turnover is what the edge is charged on.

Live baccarat NZ: the simplest good bet in the building

Baccarat requires no decisions, which makes it the easiest game to play correctly. Bet banker at 1.06%, or player at 1.24%, and never tie at 14.36%. That is the entire strategy.

Banker wins slightly more often, which is why a 5% commission is charged on it — and even after the commission it remains the better bet. No-commission variants change the banker payout on a specific winning hand to compensate, which usually makes them slightly worse; check which version the table is running.

Scorecards, roads and pattern displays are offered at nearly every live baccarat table. They record what has happened. They predict nothing, because each coup is independent, and they exist because tracking a pattern makes play feel like analysis.

How to choose a live table

  1. Read the rules panel before sitting. One zero or two; 3:2 or 6:5; commission or no commission. Thirty seconds, and it outweighs everything else.
  2. Check the limits against your bankroll. A sensible rule is a table minimum no more than about 1% of what you brought, so a run of losses does not end the session immediately.
  3. Prefer standard over branded. Multiplier, lightning and game-show versions cost more for the same nominal game.
  4. Check the bet timer. Fifteen seconds versus twenty-five changes how many rounds an hour you play, and therefore what the hour costs.
  5. Ignore the scoreboard. It is a record, not a signal.

Side bets

Every live table now carries them: perfect pairs, 21+3, lightning multipliers, dragon bonus, and so on. They are the most profitable part of the table for the operator and the most expensive part for you, typically carrying edges somewhere between 5% and 15% against a main game at 0.5% to 2.7%.

That is a specific and large number. A NZ$5 side bet alongside a NZ$25 blackjack hand can cost more in expectation than the hand itself, on a fifth of the stake. Multiplier variants of roulette work the same way: the headline multipliers are funded by reducing the base payout on straight-up numbers, so the game becomes higher-variance and higher-edge at once. The excitement is real; it is also the thing being sold.

The single most valuable habit

Play the main bet at a single-zero table with 3:2 blackjack and skip the side bets entirely. That one rule takes you from the expensive end of the table to the cheapest games in the building, and it requires no skill and no system.
