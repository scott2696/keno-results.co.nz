Live dealer games

A real table, a real dealer and a video stream — with house edges that are set by the rules on that specific table, and side bets that cost many times the main game.

Live dealer is the one part of an online casino where the outcome is not produced by a random number generator. A physical dealer runs a physical game in a studio, cameras capture it, optical recognition reads the cards or the wheel, and your bets are settled against what actually happened. The result is genuinely physical. Everything around it — the timing, the limits, the rules of that table — is a product decision, and the product decisions are where the money is.

What you are actually looking at

Most live tables are run by a handful of specialist studios rather than by the casino whose logo is on the page. The same table is frequently streamed into dozens of operators simultaneously, which has two practical consequences: the game quality is largely independent of which site you use, and the rules of that table are set by the studio, not the operator. Two sites offering “live blackjack” may be showing you the same table, or two tables whose house edges differ by a factor of three.

A round is on a timer. Betting opens, a countdown runs, betting closes, the hand or spin plays out. The pace is set by the studio and is the main reason live games generate less turnover per hour than pokies — which, since cost is charged on turnover, is a point in their favour.

House edge at the live table

These follow from the rules arithmetically. Single-zero roulette pays 35:1 against 37 pockets, so the edge is 1 ÷ 37 = 2.70%. Add a second zero and the same 35:1 runs against 38 pockets: 2 ÷ 38 = 5.26%. One extra pocket, nearly double the cost, identical-looking game.

On a single-zero wheel every bet on the layout carries the same 2.70%, because every payout is scaled to the same missing pocket. Betting a single number rather than red changes the volatility completely and the expected cost not at all — the same relationship that holds between Keno spot counts.

The rule variants that change the edge

Blackjack’s 0.5% is conditional on correct basic strategy and favourable rules. Table rules move it more than anything else you can control.

The first row is the one to look for. A 6:5 table turns the cheapest game in the casino into a mid-range one, and it is advertised identically to a 3:2 table. The payout is written on the felt — read it before you sit down, because it costs almost three times what the dealer’s hitting rule does.

And card counting does not transfer. Live blackjack shoes are typically shuffled early and often, the deck penetration is shallow, and bet-spread limits are enforced. The technique needs conditions the format is specifically arranged not to provide.

Side bets

Every live table now carries them: perfect pairs, 21+3, lightning multipliers, dragon bonus, and so on. They are the most profitable part of the table for the operator and the most expensive part for you, typically carrying edges somewhere between 5% and 15% against a main game at 0.5% to 2.7%.

That is a specific and large number. A NZ$5 side bet alongside a NZ$25 blackjack hand can cost more in expectation than the hand itself, on a fifth of the stake. Multiplier variants of roulette work the same way: the headline multipliers are funded by reducing the base payout on straight-up numbers, so the game becomes higher-variance and higher-edge at once. The excitement is real; it is also the thing being sold.

The single most valuable habit

Play the main bet at a single-zero table with 3:2 blackjack and skip the side bets entirely. That one rule takes you from the expensive end of the table to the cheapest games in the building, and it requires no skill and no system.

Game shows and wheel formats

The large-wheel and game-show formats are live dealer’s fastest growing category and its most expensive. Their house edges vary widely by segment — frequently well into double figures on the high-multiplier positions — and unlike roulette the cost is not uniform across the betting options, so the rarest-looking bet on the wheel is usually also the worst value.

Where the game publishes an RTP per segment, read it. Where it does not, assume it sits above pokie level rather than at table-game level, and treat it as entertainment priced accordingly.

Practical points before you play

  • Limits are per table. Live tables carry higher minimums than RNG versions, because a physical dealer has a cost per round. Check the minimum before sitting.
  • Connection matters. Betting closes on a timer. On an unstable connection a bet may not register, and the round proceeds regardless.
  • Bonus weighting is usually poor. Live table games commonly count 10% or less toward wagering, and are sometimes excluded entirely — which makes a bonus far more expensive to clear here than the stated multiple suggests. The arithmetic is on the bonus page.
  • Rounds are slower. Fewer rounds per hour means less turnover, and turnover is what the edge is charged on. See payouts and RTP.

Common questions

How do live dealer casino games work?
A physical dealer runs a physical game in a studio, cameras capture it, optical recognition reads the cards or wheel, and bets settle against what actually happened. Most tables are run by specialist studios and streamed into many operators at once, so the rules of a table are set by the studio rather than the casino.
Which live casino game has the lowest house edge?
Blackjack with basic strategy at a 3:2 table, at roughly 0.5%. Baccarat’s banker bet is 1.06%, single-zero roulette 2.70%, double-zero roulette 5.26%, and the baccarat tie bet 14.36%.
Are live casino side bets worth taking?
No, on the arithmetic. Side bets typically carry house edges between 5% and 15%, against 0.5% to 2.7% on the main game. A NZ$5 side bet alongside a NZ$25 blackjack hand can cost more in expectation than the hand itself.
What is the difference between 3:2 and 6:5 blackjack?
Roughly 1.4% of house edge — the largest single rule effect at the table. A 6:5 payout on a natural blackjack turns the cheapest game in the casino into a mid-range one, and it is advertised identically. The payout is written on the felt.
Can you count cards in live dealer blackjack?
Not usefully. Shoes are shuffled early and often, deck penetration is shallow, and bet-spread limits are enforced. The technique needs conditions the format is specifically arranged not to provide.
