Why there is no list of casinos on this page

Almost every page you will find for this search is a ranked list of operators, and almost every one of them is paid for. That is not a secret and it is not automatically dishonest — but it does mean the ordering is doing two jobs at once, and only one of them is yours.

This page does something narrower. Online casino advertising is prohibited in New Zealand under the Gambling Act 2003, and the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 attaches penalties of up to NZ$5 million to whoever publishes an unlawful advertisement or arranges to publish one — with Cabinet’s initial decisions naming affiliate marketing and paid endorsements specifically. So there is no shortlist here, no referral links, and nothing on this section of the site is paid for.

What is here instead is the part those lists leave out: the arithmetic. How to work out what a bonus is actually worth, what a return-to-player figure does and does not promise, what makes a withdrawal slow, and how to check whether a site may lawfully take your money at all. That is the same thing this site already does with Keno odds — publish the working rather than the conclusion.

What this section is

Editorial and unpaid. When the Department of Internal Affairs publishes its register of licensed operators, this page will report what is on the register, because a government register is a public fact. It will not become a recommendation list.

What changes on 1 December 2026

This is the thing almost no comparison page has updated for, and it matters more than any bonus on any of them.

New Zealand licensed online casino gambling for the first time in 2026. Sites that were already serving New Zealanders may continue during a transition period. That period ends on 1 December 2026, after which only operators that won the right to apply for a licence may lawfully provide online casino gambling to customers here. Licences themselves are expected from early 2027, and there will be no more than fifteen.

Key dates in the online casino licensing regime
WhenWhat happens
1 May 2026The Act comes into force. Existing operators may keep serving New Zealand, but may not advertise here.
3 July 2026Supporting regulations take effect — harm prevention, consumer protection, advertising, operator obligations.
1 December 2026The transition ends. Only operators holding the right to apply for a licence may serve New Zealand customers.
Early 2027Licences issued. Up to fifteen operators. A public register is published, and licensed sites must display a registration icon.

The practical consequence is worth stating bluntly. A recommendation written in September may point at a site that cannot lawfully take you in December. If you are reading a list that names operators and does not mention this change, the list has not been updated for the single largest thing that has happened to this market in twenty years, and I would treat everything else on it with the same caution.

How I would judge a casino

Five things, weighted in this order. The reasoning behind the weighting, and what I deliberately leave out of it, is set out in full on how we rate casinos.

  1. Legal standing in New Zealand. From December this stops being a quality signal and becomes a threshold question. An unlicensed operator leaves you with no regulator to complain to, which is the protection you are actually buying.
  2. Withdrawal behaviour. Not the advertised time — the real one, including identity verification and what triggers a hold. This is where most genuine complaints start. See withdrawals.
  3. Bonus terms, read as arithmetic. A 300% bonus at 45× wagering is worth less than a 100% bonus at 20×, and the headline number is designed to stop you working that out. See bonuses.
  4. Game range and published return figures. Named studios, disclosed RTP, and a paytable you can actually read. See payouts and RTP.
  5. Support that answers. Tested rather than assumed — and a published complaints path that does not end at the operator.

What I do not weight: bonus size on its own, “exclusive” offers, game count, and design. The first two are marketing, the third is a number anyone can inflate by counting the same game twice, and the fourth tells you nothing about whether you get paid.

The numbers that actually decide the outcome

Whatever a site looks like, the money moves according to the house edge of the games you play. These are not estimates — for the table games they fall out of the rules arithmetically, the same way Keno’s odds do.

GameHouse edgeCost per NZ$100 staked

Online pokies, typical range2–8%$2–$8
Baccarat, tie bet14.36%$14.36

Two readings of that table are worth more than any casino review.

First, the spread between the top and bottom rows is nearly thirty-fold. Choosing single-zero roulette over double-zero halves your expected cost for an otherwise identical game, and it is a choice you make at the table with no skill required. The tie bet at baccarat costs roughly fourteen times what the banker bet costs at the same table, in the same game, on the same hand.

Second, none of these numbers is a prediction about tonight. A 2.70% edge says that across a very large number of spins the wheel keeps about $2.70 of every $100 staked. Across fifty spins it says almost nothing, which is exactly why the edge is survivable as entertainment and fatal as a plan.

How online casinos differ from gambling you can already do here

New Zealanders have had legal gambling for decades, and it is worth being clear about where online casino games sit relative to it, because the differences are not the ones people expect.

The pokies in pubs and clubs are Class 4 gaming machines under the Gambling Act 2003, and they are the most tightly controlled form of gambling in the country — capped stakes, capped prizes, mandatory returns to community purposes, and venues that must exclude a player on request. Their return percentages are set by gazetted game rules rather than chosen by the venue.

The six land-based casinos, SkyCity among them, hold casino venue licences and operate under conditions the Gambling Commission has a role in. They are physical, staffed, and subject to on-site inspection.

Lotto NZ’s games — Lotto, Powerball, Keno, Bullseye, Instant Kiwi — are run by a Crown entity whose surplus goes to the Lottery Grants Board rather than to shareholders. That is why a Keno ticket and a pokie spin are not the same transaction even where the arithmetic is similar.

Online casino gambling has, until now, sat outside all of that: offered from offshore, unreachable by the regulator, and with no New Zealand complaints path. The 2026 Act is an attempt to pull it inside the tent. Whether it succeeds is a fair question, but the direction is clear, and the practical effect from December is that the set of sites lawfully able to take your money gets much smaller.

One thing does not change: prize money is not taxed in New Zealand. Lotto NZ states prizes are paid in full, and gambling winnings are generally not income for tax purposes for a recreational player. What the money earns afterwards — interest, dividends, rent — is taxable in the ordinary way. That is general information rather than tax advice.

How to check a site yourself

In the order I would do it, and none of it requires trusting a review.

Check that it may legally serve you

From December, look for the registration icon and check the Department’s public register. Before then, look at where the operator is licensed and whether that regulator publishes a searchable licence list. A licence number printed in a footer with nothing to check it against is a graphic, not a licence.

Read the bonus terms before the bonus

Find the wagering multiple, then find whether it applies to the bonus alone or to the deposit plus the bonus. Those two sentences are usually several paragraphs apart, and the gap between them can double your obligation. The bonus page has the formula and a worked example.

Find the withdrawal terms before you deposit

What documents will be required, whether there is a pending period before a withdrawal is processed, whether unprocessed withdrawals can be reversed back into your balance, and whether there is a monthly cap. A site that makes deposits instant and withdrawals a scavenger hunt has told you something.

Check the game’s own return figure

Reputable pokies publish an RTP in the game’s information panel. Some titles ship in several RTP versions and the operator chooses which to run, so the number in the panel — not the number on a review site — is the one that applies to you.

Playing safely

I run a site about a gambling product, and I think that obliges me to be direct rather than bury this in a footer. Every figure above points the same way: these games are built so that, across enough play, they keep more than they return. That is the design, not a scandal. It becomes a problem when someone plays as though it were not true.

The clearest warning sign is chasing — playing to win back a loss rather than because you wanted to play, because it means the loss is now making the decisions. The others worth taking seriously are hiding how much you play, using money meant for something else, and believing a number or a machine is due.

If any of that lands, help is free and confidential. The Gambling Helpline is 0800 654 655 or text 8006, twenty-four hours, and you can call about someone else’s gambling as easily as your own. Our getting help page has more.

How do I know if an online casino is licensed in NZ?
Two ways, from early 2027. The Department of Internal Affairs will publish a public register of licensed operators, and licensed sites must display a unique registration icon. The register is the authoritative check — an icon that does not match a register entry is just an image.
