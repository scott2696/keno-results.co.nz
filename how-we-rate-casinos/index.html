What Is Paid for, and What Is Not

The operator table appears at the top of every page in this section, including this one. Every row in it is a paid placement, it is labelled as one in the table itself, and it appears in an order this site chose.

That order used to be alphabetical, and these pages said so, because alphabetical is a claim a reader can verify in about five seconds. It is now a sequence set by hand, so that verification is gone — and the honest move is to say so rather than keep the sentence and hope nobody checks. Treat the order as what it is: an editorial decision on a table of paid placements. Everything below it is still unpaid, and that is the part worth testing.

What is not paid for is every word underneath it. The criteria below, the house edge figures, the bonus arithmetic and the withdrawal mechanics are unchanged by who is in that table, and no partner has any input into them, sees a page before it is published, or is told what it will say.

There is a test for whether that means anything, and I would rather name it than ask to be believed. There is also a limit worth stating plainly. Online casino advertising is prohibited in New Zealand under the Gambling Act 2003, and the penalties reach whoever publishes an unlawful advertisement rather than only the operator, with affiliate marketing named specifically in the advertising decisions. That is an open question for this site rather than a settled one, and I would rather say so here than pretend the question does not exist. How to Choose an Online Casino NZ: How to Tell If One Is Legit

1. Casino Licensing Explained: How to Check If a Casino Is Licensed in NZ

Weighted first because it is not really a quality signal at all — it is a threshold. An operator with no verifiable licence is not a worse version of a licensed one; it is a different kind of thing, with no regulator to complain to and no enforceable obligations behind anything it promises. Everything else on this list is a matter of degree. This one is binary.

What I check, and what takes about two minutes: find the registered company and the named regulator in the footer — the company rather than the brand, since one company often runs several. Go to that regulator’s own site, not the casino’s, and search its public register for the company. Confirm the entry is current and covers online casino gambling.

A licence number printed in a footer is a graphic. A seal that is an image rather than a link to a register entry is a graphic. Anything can be typed onto a web page; a register entry cannot. That distinction is the whole check.

2. Withdrawal Behaviour

Second because it is where almost every real complaint originates, and because it is the one thing a site can get wrong that costs you money you have already won.

What matters is not the advertised processing time. It is the identity verification requirements and when they are applied, whether there is a pending period, whether a pending withdrawal can be reversed back into your playable balance, and whether there are caps. Withdrawals covers each of those.

3. Bonus Terms, Read as Arithmetic

Third because it is the criterion most comparison sites invert. They weight bonus size, which is the number the operator controls and the one designed to stop you calculating. The useful question is whether the offer has positive expected value, and that is a division problem — set out in full on casino bonuses.

4. Games and Published Return Figures

Fourth, and measured by disclosure rather than by count. A site that publishes the return-to-player figure in each game’s information panel, names the studios, and shows a readable paytable is doing something a site with “7,000+ games” in a banner is not. Game counts are trivially inflated by counting variants; RTP disclosure is not.

5. Support and Complaints Path

Fifth, and judged on whether there is somewhere to go when the operator says no. An internal complaints team is not a complaints path. A named external regulator or dispute service, with a published process, is.

Why There Is No Score Out of Ten

Every competitor publishes one. This site does not, and the reason is not modesty.

A composite score compresses several incomparable things — licensing, withdrawal behaviour, bonus value, game range — into one number using weights that are almost never published. The compression destroys information. An operator that pays reliably but has a mediocre game library and one that has a superb library and a withdrawal problem can produce the same 8.4, and the 8.4 tells you nothing about which is which.

It also invites a specific abuse: when the weights are private, they can be adjusted until the ordering matches the commercial arrangements, and nobody outside can tell. A score is the most persuasive format available and the least checkable, which is exactly the wrong combination for a page that carries paid placement.

So this section publishes criteria and arithmetic instead, and leaves the weighing to you. It is less convenient. It is auditable.

What Would Change My Mind

Our Online Casino Review Methodology: Where the Numbers Come From

House edge and probability figures in this section are derived from the published rules of the game, not quoted from another site. Single-zero roulette’s 2.70% edge is 1 ÷ 37; the break-even wagering multiple on the bonus page is 1 ÷ house edge. Where a figure cannot be derived or sourced, it does not appear. That is why you will not find withdrawal times or payment-method availability asserted here as facts — they vary by operator and by month, and repeating them from another comparison site is how an unverified claim becomes an industry consensus.

The same standard runs through the rest of the site. Every Keno draw published here is validated before it goes live, and the return calculator refuses to ship a baked-in paytable precisely because a precise number from an invented source is worse than no number.

How to Spot a Casino Review You Should Not Trust

Applying a method to this site is only useful if you can apply it to the next one too. These are the tells, and they are all visible without leaving the page.

  • A score with no working. “9.2/10” built from unstated weights on unstated inputs is a number wearing the costume of a measurement.
  • Ranking by bonus size. The arithmetic on casino bonuses shows bonus size cancels out of whether an offer is good. A list ordered by it is ordered by the one variable that does not matter.
  • Claimed testing with no detail. “We deposited and withdrew” without amounts, dates, methods or elapsed time is a claim structured so it cannot be checked.
  • No dates anywhere. Offers and payment methods change monthly. An undated page is a claim about one week, published as though permanent.
  • Every operator recommended. If nothing is ever criticised, the page is a directory of commercial relationships rather than an assessment.
  • Disclosure only in the footer. Compare where the commercial relationship is disclosed with where the call to action sits. The distance between them is the intent.
  • Numbers with no source. Payout percentages and withdrawal times asserted flatly. Ask where a figure came from; on most pages there is no answer because it was copied from the page above it in the results.

Responsible Gambling NZ and the Gambling Helpline

A method for choosing a casino is incomplete without this, so it sits in the method rather than in a footer.

Every figure published in this section points the same way: these games are built so that, across enough play, they return less than they take in. That is the design, disclosed and legal. It becomes a problem when someone plays as though it were not true — and the clearest signal of that is chasing, playing to win back a loss rather than because you wanted to play, because it means the loss is now making the decisions.

Practical limits that work better than willpower: set a deposit limit inside the account before you play, since most platforms impose a cooling-off period before one can be raised; use a prepaid method so a session is capped by what you bought; remove stored cards so each deposit takes a deliberate act; and ask your bank whether it will block gambling merchant categories, because several New Zealand banks will.

Responsible gambling NZ support is free and confidential. The Gambling Helpline NZ is 0800 654 655 or text 8006, twenty-four hours, and you can call about someone else’s gambling as easily as your own — you do not need their permission or involvement, and you do not need to have worked out what to say first. Safer Gambling Aotearoa has further options, and our getting help page has more. There is also a responsible gambling page covering the Keno side of this site.
