Every Keno multiplier we have recorded

Across 220 draws the multiplier averaged x2.03. Here is the full distribution, why it is observation rather than a published schedule, and why it never changes your odds.

Every NZ Keno draw carries a multiplier that scales what a winning ticket pays. Lotto NZ publish it with each result, but not, as far as we can find, the probabilities behind it. So we counted.

Across the 220 draws in our archive:

Observed Keno multiplier frequency across 220 draws
MultiplierDrawsShareRoughly
×1.511954.1%1 in 1.8
×26830.9%1 in 3.2
×3219.5%1 in 10.5
×5104.5%1 in 22
×1020.9%1 in 110

The mean works out at ×2.03. More than half of all draws carry the smallest multiplier, and ×10 showed up twice in nearly two months.

What this is not

It is not a published schedule. These are counts from a limited window, and the operator sets the real weightings. A larger sample would move these figures — treat the shape as indicative, not exact.

It is also not predictable. The multiplier on the next draw is unaffected by recent ones. A long run without a ×10 does not make one more likely, in exactly the way a long absence does not make a number due.

It does not change your odds

This is the part worth being clear about. The multiplier changes what a win pays. It has no effect whatsoever on how likely a win is. Your chance of matching numbers depends only on how many spots you play — see the full odds tables — and is identical on a ×1.5 draw and a ×10 draw.

Because it is drawn before the result and applies to everyone in that draw equally, there is also no way to select for it.
