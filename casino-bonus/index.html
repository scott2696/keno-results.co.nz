A casino bonus is not free money and it is not a scam. It is a trade: the operator gives you funds you cannot withdraw, in exchange for a volume of betting you would not otherwise have done. Whether that trade is worth taking depends on two numbers, and you can settle it before you claim anything.

What a wagering requirement is

A wagering requirement — also called a playthrough or rollover — is how much you must stake before bonus funds become withdrawable. It is quoted as a multiple, and it applies either to the bonus alone or to the deposit plus the bonus. That distinction is the single most expensive detail in the terms, and the two sentences establishing it are usually several paragraphs apart.

Turnover required = bonus amount × wagering multiple

Take a NZ$100 deposit with a 100% match and 40× wagering on the bonus. The bonus is NZ$100, so you must stake NZ$4,000 before you can withdraw. If the same offer applies 40× to deposit plus bonus, the base is NZ$200 and the obligation is NZ$8,000. Identical headline, double the work.

What that turnover costs you

Staking money at a casino has an expected cost: the house edge, multiplied by everything you stake. Online pokies commonly run somewhere near a 4% edge, so every NZ$100 staked costs about NZ$4 in expectation.

Apply that to the example. Wagering NZ$4,000 at a 4% edge has an expected cost of NZ$160. The bonus was NZ$100. On those terms the offer has negative expected value before you account for game weighting, maximum bet rules or expiry — you are paying NZ$160 of expectation to receive NZ$100.

The break-even multiple

That leads to the only number worth memorising here. A bonus breaks even when the expected cost of the turnover equals the bonus itself:

bonus × multiple × house edge = bonus
break-even multiple = 1 ÷ house edge

The bonus amount cancels out entirely. How big the bonus is has no bearing on whether it is a good one — only the multiple and the edge of the games you play on it do. That is why a site ranking offers by size is ranking them by the one variable that does not matter.

Why the table is not the whole answer

Read the left-hand column again. The low-edge games have the highest break-even multiples, which looks backwards until you see what is happening: cheap games take longer to convert. And this is precisely where game weighting arrives to close the loophole.

Game weighting

Almost every bonus counts different games differently toward the requirement. Pokies typically count 100%. Table games often count 10%, sometimes 5%, sometimes zero. A blackjack player facing a nominal 40× at 10% weighting is really facing 400× of blackjack turnover — far past the 200× break-even, and the reason the arbitrage in the table above does not exist in practice.

Read weighting as a multiplier on the requirement: effective multiple = stated multiple ÷ weighting. Any game weighted at 0% cannot clear the bonus at all, and staking on it is pure cost.

Maximum bet while wagering

Commonly NZ$5 to NZ$8 per spin or hand. Exceed it once, even accidentally, and most terms allow the operator to void the bonus and everything won with it. This is the single most common reason a player who cleared a bonus does not get paid, and it is enforced automatically.

Expiry

Seven days versus thirty changes whether the turnover is achievable at all. NZ$4,000 of turnover in seven days at NZ$2 a spin is 2,000 spins, which is a real time commitment rather than a technicality. Work out the spins, not just the dollars.

Maximum conversion

A cap on what bonus winnings can become — often expressed as a multiple of the bonus, sometimes as a flat figure. If you clear a NZ$100 bonus with a 5× conversion cap and are sitting on NZ$3,000, you withdraw NZ$500. The cap truncates the upside while leaving the turnover obligation intact, which makes a capped bonus materially worse than an uncapped one at the same multiple.

The five terms that decide whether an offer is any good

  1. The multiple, and what it applies to — bonus only, or deposit plus bonus.
  2. Game weighting — divide the multiple by the weighting of what you actually play.
  3. Maximum bet while wagering — the rule most likely to void everything.
  4. Expiry — convert the turnover into spins at your normal stake, then decide.
  5. Maximum conversion — the ceiling on what you can actually take out.

A worked example, end to end

An offer of 200% up to NZ$500, 35× wagering on bonus only, pokies weighted 100%, max bet NZ$5, 30 days, 10× conversion cap. You deposit NZ$250 and receive NZ$500 in bonus.

  • Turnover: NZ$500 × 35 = NZ$17,500.
  • Expected cost at a 4% edge: NZ$17,500 × 0.04 = NZ$700.
  • Value received: NZ$500.
  • Expected net: −NZ$200, before variance.
  • Spins at NZ$5: 3,500. At NZ$2: 8,750.

35× against a 25× break-even makes this a negative-expectation offer, and the 200% headline is the largest number in it and the least relevant. The same operator offering 50% up to NZ$125 at 20× would be handing you a genuinely positive-value bonus, and almost nobody would click it.

The honest summary

Most advertised welcome bonuses carry wagering above the break-even line, which means they are marketing costs recovered from turnover rather than gifts. That is not a reason never to take one — a bonus extends playing time and the entertainment is the point. It is a reason to stop treating bonus size as a quality signal, and to know which side of 25× a specific offer sits on.

A note on expected value

Every figure here is a long-run average, not a prediction. Negative expected value does not mean you will lose exactly NZ$200; it means that is the average outcome across a very large number of repetitions of this exact situation. Any single run can land anywhere. Decide your limit before you start, and see getting help if gambling stops being fun.

Common questions

What does 40x wagering actually mean?
That you must stake forty times the bonus before bonus funds become withdrawable. On a NZ$100 bonus that is NZ$4,000 of turnover — or NZ$8,000 if the multiple applies to deposit plus bonus rather than to the bonus alone.
At what wagering requirement is a casino bonus worth taking?
The break-even multiple is 1 divided by the house edge, and the bonus amount cancels out entirely. On a typical pokie at a 4% edge that is 25×. Below 25× the offer has positive expected value; above it you are paying more in expected turnover cost than the bonus is worth.
Is a bigger casino bonus better?
No — bonus size has no bearing on whether an offer is good, because it cancels out of the break-even calculation. Only the wagering multiple, the game weighting and the house edge of what you play decide it. A 50% bonus at 20× beats a 200% bonus at 35×.
What is game weighting on a bonus?
The proportion of each dollar staked that counts toward the requirement. Pokies usually count 100%, table games often 10% or less. Divide the stated multiple by the weighting to get the effective one: a nominal 40× at 10% weighting is really 400×.
Why was my bonus voided?
Most commonly for exceeding the maximum bet while wagering — usually around NZ$5 to NZ$8 per spin or hand. It only takes one bet, it is enforced automatically, and most terms allow the operator to void the bonus and everything won with it.
