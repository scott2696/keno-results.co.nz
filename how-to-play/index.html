How to play Keno

Keno is one of the simplest lottery games to understand: pick some numbers, wait for the draw, count your matches. Here is the whole game.

The game in one paragraph

Every draw, 20 numbers are selected at random from a pool of 1 to 80. Before the draw you choose between one and ten numbers of your own — these are your spots. Your prize depends on how many of your spots appear among the 20 drawn, and on how much you staked.

Playing a ticket

  1. Choose how many spots to play. Anywhere from one to ten. This is the most consequential decision you make, because it determines the entire prize structure of your ticket.
  2. Choose your numbers. Mark them yourself, or take a random selection — often called a Dip. Neither method affects your odds; see the odds page for why.
  3. Set your stake. Prizes scale with what you put on.
  4. Choose how many draws to enter. One, or the same numbers across several consecutive draws.
  5. Keep your ticket. It is proof of entry. You need it to claim.

How prizes are structured

Each spot count has its own prize table. A six-spot ticket typically pays something for three or more matches; a ten-spot ticket pays over a wider spread of outcomes but needs more matches for the top tier.

The pattern worth understanding: more spots does not mean better odds. Matching all ten on a ten-spot ticket is far harder than matching all four on a four-spot ticket. What more spots buys you is a longer ladder of smaller prizes.

Things people get wrong

"Some numbers are due"

They are not. Each draw is independent, and the machine has no memory of previous draws. A number that has not appeared in fifty draws is exactly as likely to appear next draw as any other.

"Picking unpopular numbers improves my odds"

It does not change your odds of winning. In games with shared pools it can affect how much you would share a prize with others, but the chance of matching is identical either way.

"There is a system"

There is not. See the odds for the arithmetic.

Playing sensibly

Keno has a fixed house edge, which means that over time the game returns less than it takes in. That is how it is designed. Treat what you spend as the cost of the entertainment rather than an investment, decide your limit before you start, and stop there.
