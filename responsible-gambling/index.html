Responsible gambling

Keno is designed to return less than it takes in. Knowing exactly how, and what that means for you, is the most useful thing this site can tell you.

If you need help now

Free, confidential, 24/7 support is available in New Zealand from the Gambling Helpline Aotearoa. They also support family and friends of people affected by gambling. Contacting them costs nothing and does not commit you to anything.

The house edge is not a rumour

Every Keno prize tier pays less than its true odds would justify. That difference is the house edge, and it is what funds the operator, the retailers and the community grants. It is a design feature, disclosed and legal — not a flaw and not a conspiracy.

What it means practically: the longer you play, the more certain it becomes that you finish behind. Short sessions are volatile and you may well win. Long-run play converges on the edge, and the edge never moves.

No selection method changes it. Not hot numbers, not cold numbers, not systems, not spreading across more spots. See the odds for the arithmetic, and our frequency page for what randomness actually looks like over 220 draws.

Four draws a day

Keno draws roughly every few hours, four times daily. That frequency is the part most worth watching — it is what turns an occasional flutter into something with a rhythm. If you set limits, set them per day or per week, never per draw. A limit that resets four times a day is not a limit.

Signs worth taking seriously

  • Playing more, or for longer, than you intended.
  • Chasing a loss — playing again specifically to recover money.
  • Gambling with money set aside for something else.
  • Hiding how much you play, or lying about it.
  • Feeling anxious, irritable or low when you cannot play.
  • Borrowing to fund play.
  • Believing you are "due" a win.

That last one is worth dwelling on, because this site can disprove it directly. Each draw is independent. A number absent for fifty draws is exactly as likely to appear as any other, and so is a person who has lost fifty times. Nothing is owed to anyone.

Practical limits

  • Decide what you are prepared to lose before you start, and treat it as the price of the entertainment.
  • Set a time limit as well as a money limit.
  • Never gamble to make money, solve a problem, or recover a loss.
  • Do not play while upset, bored or drinking.
  • Take real breaks — days, not minutes.
  • Keep it social and occasional rather than routine and solitary.

Where to get help

  • Gambling Helpline Aotearoa — free, confidential, 24/7, for gamblers and for whanau and friends.
  • Ministry of Health — publishes the list of funded problem-gambling services across New Zealand.
  • Department of Internal Affairs — regulates gambling in New Zealand.
  • Lotto NZ offer self-exclusion and account limits on their own platform. We cannot set these for you — we are not the operator.

Under 18

You must be 18 or over to buy a lottery product in New Zealand. This site is intended for the same audience.

Where we stand

We publish results and explain odds. We do not sell tickets, take stakes, run promotions, or earn anything when you play. That independence is why we can say plainly what an operator would phrase more gently:

Keno is entertainment with a price. It is not an income, an investment, or a plan. If it has stopped being fun, that is the signal — not a run of bad luck.
