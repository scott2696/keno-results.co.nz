Powerball NZ

How Powerball attaches to a Lotto line, and what it does to the odds.

How it works

Powerball is not a standalone game. You add it to a Lotto line, and a single extra number is drawn from a separate, smaller pool. To take the Powerball jackpot you need all six of your Lotto numbers and the Powerball number.

What it does to your odds

Because the Powerball is drawn independently from its own pool, the chance of taking the top prize is the chance of matching six Lotto numbers multiplied by the chance of matching the Powerball. That makes the jackpot substantially harder to win than Lotto's own first division — which is precisely why the prize is larger.

Powerball also adds its own lower tiers, so a line can win on the Powerball number alongside fewer Lotto matches.

Jackpots

When nobody takes the top prize it rolls over, which is how Powerball reaches the very large jackpots it is known for. A rollover does not change your odds — it changes only the size of the prize you are playing for.

A common misreading

A bigger jackpot does not mean a better chance. The odds of any given line are identical whether the jackpot is at its minimum or at a record high. What changes is the payout, not the probability.

For current jackpot sizes, draw dates and prize schedules, check Lotto New Zealand directly. We publish Keno results; we do not carry live Powerball data.
