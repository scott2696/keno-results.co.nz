Gaming in New Zealand

The online casino law change

New Zealand licensed online casino gambling for the first time in 2026. Here is what actually changes, when, and what it means if you already play on an offshore site.

This is the biggest change to New Zealand gambling law in two decades, and most of the coverage I read while researching it was either wrong about the dates or quietly selling something. So I went to the legislation and the regulator instead. Everything below is linked to a source.

What the Act does

The Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 came into force on 1 May 2026. Before it, online casino gambling occupied an odd space: it was not lawful for anyone to offer it from within New Zealand, but New Zealanders playing on offshore sites were not breaking the law themselves, and the offshore operators were effectively out of reach. The Act creates a licensing regime so that online casino gambling can be offered here legally, under conditions, by a limited number of operators.

The dates that matter

Online Casino Gambling Act timeline
WhenWhat happens
1 May 2026The Act comes into force. Operators already serving New Zealand may continue, but must not advertise here.
3 July 2026The supporting regulations take effect, covering harm prevention, consumer protection, advertising and operator obligations.
1 December 2026The transition ends. Only operators that won the right to apply for a licence at auction may provide online casino gambling to customers in New Zealand.
Early 2027The Department of Internal Affairs expects to begin issuing licences. Up to 15 operators in total.

I want to be precise about one thing the secondary coverage keeps blurring. 1 December 2026 is when unlicensed operators must stop serving New Zealand customers. Early 2027 is when licences are actually issued. Those are different milestones, and the gap between them is real.

What changes for you as a player

Right now, nothing. The regulator has been explicit that there are no changes for online casino customers during the transition. From 1 December the practical change is that the set of sites lawfully able to take your money shrinks to the licensed group.

Two things will make a licensed operator identifiable. The Department will publish a public register of licensed online casinos, and licensed operators will have to display a unique registration icon on their platform and in their advertising. If a site cannot show either, it is not licensed here.

The reason to care is not moral, it is practical: on an unlicensed platform you do not get the protections the regime is built to provide, and you have no regulator to complain to. With a licensed operator you can complain to the casino or to the Department directly.

What changes for advertising — including this site

Online casino advertising has been prohibited in New Zealand all along, under the Gambling Act 2003. What the new Act adds is enforcement: the Department can issue takedown notices and seek penalties of up to NZ$5 million, and those apply to whoever publishes or arranges to publish the advertisement, not only to the operator. Cabinet’s initial decisions on the advertising regulations specifically name affiliate marketing and paid endorsements.

I am saying that plainly because it applies to me. This site carries paid placements from gambling operators elsewhere, always labelled. I have kept them off this section entirely, and the change is one I am working through rather than one I can pretend does not exist.

Sports betting is not affected

Betting on sport and racing is regulated separately, under the Racing Industry Act 2020 and the Gambling Act 2003, through TAB NZ. The online casino changes do not alter that. It is a common confusion and worth keeping straight: a law change about online casinos says nothing about a bet on a rugby match.

Not legal advice

I am not a lawyer. This is a plain reading of the Act, the regulations and the Department of Internal Affairs’ published guidance, with links so you can check each claim. If you are making a business decision on the back of it, get proper advice.
