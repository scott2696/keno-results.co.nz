Lotto New Zealand operates several games. Keno is the one this site covers in detail; the others are summarised here for context.

Lotto

Six numbers drawn from 1 to 40, plus a bonus ball. A standard line matches against those six. Prize tiers run from three matches upward, with the top tier requiring all six.

Powerball

An add-on to a Lotto line: an extra number drawn from a separate smaller pool. Matching all six Lotto numbers plus the Powerball takes the jackpot. Because it is a second independent draw, adding Powerball lengthens the odds on the top prize considerably.

Bullseye

A daily game with a different structure again — a single number drawn, with prizes based on how closely your entry matches.

Instant Kiwi

Scratch tickets. There is no draw: the outcome is fixed when the ticket is printed, and the published odds describe the whole print run rather than a per-draw probability.

Keno

Twenty numbers drawn from 1 to 80, with players choosing one to ten spots. It has more frequent draws than Lotto and a wider spread of prize tiers. See how to play and the odds.

Strike

A second add-on to a Lotto line. Where Lotto asks only which numbers came out, Strike asks for the first four in the exact order they were drawn. Same draw, same numbers, a harder question — which is why it carries its own prize pool.

The Odds, Worked Through

A Lotto line is one combination of six numbers from forty. That is C(40,6) = 3,838,380, so a single line takes First Division about once in 3.84 million. Adding Powerball multiplies that by the size of the Powerball pool:

Every figure there follows from the rules of the game and can be checked on a calculator. We publish arithmetic rather than rounded marketing numbers, the same way we do on the Keno odds tables.

Draw Days

Lotto, Powerball and Strike are drawn on Wednesday and Saturday evenings. Keno draws four times every day and Bullseye once daily, so if you are used to Keno’s rhythm, Lotto is a much slower game. The latest Lotto result is at the top of this page.

How Long You Have to Claim

Lotto NZ allows 12 months from the draw date to claim a prize on any of its draw games. Instant Kiwi is different because there is no draw date: the 12 months run from the date the game closes, not from when you bought the ticket. After that the prize is forfeited, which is how large unclaimed prizes occasionally end up in the news.

Tax on Winnings

Lotto NZ states that prize money from Lotto and Powerball is not taxed, so a prize is paid in full. What is taxable is what the money then earns — interest, dividends, rent — in the ordinary way. That is general information rather than tax advice; Inland Revenue or an accountant is the right answer for your own circumstances.

“Most Common” Lotto Numbers

A common search, and worth answering plainly: some numbers have come up more often than others, and that tells you nothing about the next draw. Each draw is independent, the machine has no memory, and a number that has appeared fifty times is exactly as likely as one that has appeared thirty. Over a long enough run the counts converge — we show that happening with real Keno data on the statistics pages, where a spread of 100 points across five draws collapses to 15 across 220.

The one honest use for frequency data is avoiding numbers other people favour. It will not improve your chance of winning, but if you do win on an unpopular combination you are less likely to share the prize.
