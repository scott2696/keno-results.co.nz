The official operator

Keno in New Zealand is run by Lotto New Zealand, the trading name of the New Zealand Lotteries Commission — a Crown entity. They are the only body legally selling Keno tickets to New Zealanders.

There are two ways to buy:

  • In person, from a Lotto retailer — dairies, supermarkets, service stations and Lotto shops nationwide.
  • Online or in-app, through MyLotto, the operator's own platform, which requires a verified account.

Profits go to the New Zealand Lottery Grants Board and are distributed to community causes. That is a genuine difference from offshore gambling sites, where nothing returns to New Zealand.

Age and eligibility

You must be 18 or over to buy a lottery product in New Zealand. Online purchase requires identity verification, and a New Zealand bank account for withdrawals.

Offshore sites and casino Keno

Plenty of offshore operators offer a game called Keno. Two things are worth understanding before going anywhere near one.

It is usually a different game. Casino Keno is typically a software RNG title with its own paytable, sometimes a different pool size, and frequently a worse return than a state lottery product. The odds tables on this site describe New Zealand Keno, 20 drawn from 80. They do not describe whatever a casino has labelled Keno.

Your protections differ. Under New Zealand law it is not an offence for an individual to gamble on an overseas website, but those operators are outside the jurisdiction of the Department of Internal Affairs. If something goes wrong — a withheld withdrawal, a disputed result, a closed account — the Gambling Act 2003 and New Zealand's regulator do not help you. Whatever the site's own terms say is what you get.

What to check anywhere

  • Who holds the licence, and whether the regulator is one you could actually reach.
  • The paytable for that specific game — not a generic Keno table found elsewhere. Run it through our return calculator to see the real house edge.
  • Deposit and loss limits, and whether they can be lowered instantly rather than after a cooling-off period.
  • Self-exclusion, and how it is enforced.
  • Withdrawal terms — particularly wagering requirements attached to any bonus.

Before you play anywhere

Keno carries a fixed house edge, and it draws four times a day. Decide your limit before you start and set it per day or per week rather than per draw. If gambling is causing harm, free and confidential help is available from the Gambling Helpline Aotearoa — see responsible gambling.
