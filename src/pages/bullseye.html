How It Works

Bullseye draws a single six-digit number, anywhere from 000000 to 999999, once a day. You are not matching a set of numbers against a larger drawn set the way you are in Lotto or Keno — there is one drawn outcome, and your entry is measured against it. That is the whole structure, and it is what makes the game unlike anything else Lotto NZ runs.

The Arithmetic of an Exact Match

Six digits, each 0 to 9, gives 106 = 1,000,000 possible numbers. So a single entry matches the drawn number exactly once in a million. That figure follows from the format alone, it is not an estimate, and it does not move.

What we do not publish is the prize ladder for partial matches. Lotto NZ sets those values and can change them, and a stale prize table is worse than none — someone plans around it. For current prizes and the divisions below the top one, check the operator.

How It Differs From Keno

Keno gives you control over how many numbers you play, and your prize ladder follows from that choice — see the odds tables for all ten spot counts. Bullseye has no such decision: the structure is fixed, and the only variable is your stake. Keno also draws four times a day against Bullseye's one.

Draw Schedule

Bullseye is drawn daily, in the evening, New Zealand time. Every draw we hold has landed just after 6pm, which is consistent with Keno's last draw of the day — but Bullseye is a single daily draw, not one of four. Our draw times page covers the Keno schedule in detail.

Can You Improve Your Chances?

No. Every six-digit number is exactly as likely as every other, a number that came up yesterday is no less likely today, and there is no pattern in past draws to read — that is what random means. The only thing you decide is how much to stake.

Scope
