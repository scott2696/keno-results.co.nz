How Keno prizes work

What determines the size of a Keno win in New Zealand — and why we publish the structure rather than a prize table.

{subnav}

Four things set your prize

  1. How many spots you played. Each spot count has its own prize ladder. See the odds tables for every tier from 1 to 10 spots.
  2. How many you matched. Your tier within that ladder.
  3. Your stake. Keno lets you choose your wager, and the prize scales with it. A $2 ticket pays twice what the same result pays on a $1 ticket.
  4. The multiplier on that draw. Every draw carries one, and it multiplies the prize for a winning ticket. We have recorded values from ×1.5 to ×10 — see the multiplier page.

So the same three matched numbers can be worth very different amounts depending on the ticket that produced them. That is why no single prize table can answer "how much did I win".

The cap

New Zealand Keno has a maximum payout per ticket — publicly stated as NZ$1 million. Wagers run from NZ$1 to NZ$100 per ticket. Confirm both with Lotto NZ before relying on them; operators change these.

Why we do not publish a prize table

Our position

Prize values are set by Lotto NZ and can change without notice. Publishing a table we cannot guarantee would be worse than publishing none — someone would plan around a stale figure.

What we can publish, and do, is the odds. Those are fixed by the rules of the game, identical for every player, and arithmetically checkable. See our editorial standards.

Working out whether a ticket is worth it

The honest way to think about a Keno ticket is not "what could I win" but "what does this cost on average". Keno carries a fixed house edge, which means the expected return on any ticket is less than the stake — that is the design, and no spot count, number choice or multiplier changes it.

The multiplier is the most commonly misread part. It changes what a win pays, never how likely a win is. A ×10 draw and a ×1.5 draw have identical odds.

Claiming

Prizes are claimed through Lotto NZ, not here. Smaller amounts can usually be claimed at a retailer; larger ones go through the operator and may need identification. Prizes expire after a set period, so check the current claim window with them rather than assuming. More on our rules page.

A match here is not a claim

Our ticket checker tells you how many numbers matched. It does not calculate a prize and confers no entitlement. Always confirm a win with Lotto NZ.
