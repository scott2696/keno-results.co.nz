Licensed online casinos and the 2026 register

Which operators may lawfully serve New Zealanders from 1 December 2026, how the public register and the registration icon will work, and what a licence does and does not protect.

{subnav}

For the whole history of online casino gambling in New Zealand there has been no such thing as a licensed online casino here. That changes in stages across 2026 and 2027, and the stages are easy to confuse — most of the coverage I read while writing this conflated at least two of them. Everything below is linked to a source.

The dates

Key dates in the New Zealand online casino licensing regime
WhenWhat happens
1 May 2026The Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 comes into force. Operators already serving New Zealand may continue during the transition, but may not advertise here.
3 July 2026The supporting regulations take effect, covering harm prevention, consumer protection, advertising and operator obligations.
1 December 2026The transition ends. Only operators that won the right to apply for a licence may provide online casino gambling to customers in New Zealand.
Early 2027The Department of Internal Affairs expects to begin issuing licences. Up to fifteen operators in total.

The distinction worth holding onto: 1 December 2026 is when unlicensed operators must stop serving New Zealand customers. Early 2027 is when licences are actually issued. Those are different milestones and there is a real gap between them, during which the operators permitted to serve you are those holding the right to apply, not those holding a licence.

Why the licences were auctioned

The number of licences is capped at fifteen, and the right to apply for one was allocated by auction rather than by assessment alone. That is an unusual design and the reasoning is worth understanding: a capped, contested market gives the regulator leverage it has never had over offshore operators, because a licence has value that can be withdrawn.

Winning at auction is not the same as being licensed. An operator must still satisfy the Department on suitability and on its harm-prevention and consumer-protection obligations before a licence issues.

How to check whether a site is licensed

Two mechanisms, and both are designed to be checkable by a player rather than by a lawyer.

The public register

The Department of Internal Affairs will publish a register of licensed online casino operators. A register is the authoritative source: if a site is not on it, it is not licensed here, whatever its footer says.

The registration icon

Licensed operators will be required to display a unique registration icon on their platform and in any advertising. The icon is the quick check; the register is the one that settles it. Treat an icon that does not correspond to a register entry as worth exactly nothing — an image on a web page is trivially copied, and a register lookup is not.

Until the register exists

Before licensing, the useful question is where an operator is licensed elsewhere and whether that regulator publishes a searchable licence list you can check the company name against. A licence number printed in a footer with nothing to verify it against is a graphic, not a licence. See how we rate casinos.

What a New Zealand licence actually gives you

The honest answer is that it gives you a regulator, and almost everything else follows from that.

  • A complaints path that does not end at the operator. You will be able to complain to the casino or to the Department directly. This is the concrete change, and it is the one worth most — on an unlicensed platform, an internal complaints team is the last stop.
  • Enforceable harm-prevention obligations. The regulations cover harm prevention and consumer protection as licence conditions, which makes them enforceable rather than aspirational.
  • A licence that can be lost. Fifteen capped licences in a market means an operator has something to protect. That is a stronger incentive than any code of conduct.
  • Advertising subject to rules. The regulations govern how licensed operators may advertise, with penalties for breach.

What it does not give you

Worth being equally clear about, because licensing is routinely oversold.

  • Better odds. A licence does not change the house edge. A licensed pokie and an unlicensed one can run the identical game at the identical return — see payouts and RTP.
  • A guarantee you are paid. It gives you somewhere to escalate. It does not override the operator’s terms, and most withdrawal refusals turn on a term the player breached — see withdrawals.
  • Protection from your own limits. Licensing conditions require tools; they cannot require you to use them.
  • Safety of your deposit if an operator fails. Whether player funds are segregated is a question about the operator’s arrangements, and worth asking separately.

What happens to players on unlicensed sites

The Act regulates operators and advertisers rather than criminalising players. A New Zealander who plays on an unlicensed offshore site is not committing an offence by doing so.

What you lose is the regulator. There is no New Zealand body to complain to, no enforceable local obligation on the operator, and no register entry to check — and from December, the operator is also doing something it is not permitted to do, which is not a promising foundation for a dispute about a withdrawal.

What changes for advertising — including this site

Online casino advertising has been prohibited in New Zealand all along, under the Gambling Act 2003. What the 2026 Act adds is enforcement: the Department can issue takedown notices and seek penalties of up to NZ$5 million, and those reach whoever publishes or arranges to publish the advertisement, not only the operator. Cabinet’s initial decisions on the advertising regulations name affiliate marketing and paid endorsements specifically.

Sports betting is not affected

Betting on sport and racing is regulated separately, under the Racing Industry Act 2020 alongside the Gambling Act 2003, through TAB NZ. The online casino changes do not alter it. It is a common confusion and worth keeping straight: a law change about online casinos says nothing about a bet on a rugby match.

Nor does it affect Lotto NZ’s games. Keno, Lotto, Powerball, Bullseye and Instant Kiwi are run by a Crown entity under its own authority, and sit outside this regime entirely — see who regulates gambling.
