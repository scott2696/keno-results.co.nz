What this can and cannot tell you

Each Keno draw is independent, and the machine has no memory of previous draws. A number that has come up in 80% of the last five draws is exactly as likely to appear in the next one as a number that has not appeared at all — both sit at 25%, every draw, always.

Short windows swing widely for that reason. Over five draws a number can easily show at 80% or 0% through ordinary chance; widen the window and everything drifts back toward 25%. Try it with the selector above — watching the spread narrow as you go from 5 draws to 250 is the clearest demonstration that these are not signals.

Presenting frequency without implying prediction is one of our editorial standards.

    What these tools are for

    Every number on this page is counted from the draws in our own archive, which comes from Lotto NZ and is validated before anything is stored. Nothing here is modelled, smoothed or predicted — it is a count, and you can reproduce it from the draw archive yourself.

    Hot and cold numbers

    The selector at the top counts how often each number appeared across the last 5, 10, 25, 50, 100 or 250 draws. The window matters more than the numbers: at five draws you are looking at 100 balls in total, so a single number appearing twice looks dramatic and means nothing. At 250 draws you are looking at 5,000 balls and the spread flattens out.

    That flattening is the actual finding, and it is why the selector is the most useful control on the page. We wrote it up with real figures in what 220 draws say about hot numbers: the gap between the most and least drawn number falls from about 100 points across five draws to 15 across 220.

    Frequency, gaps and pairs

    Beyond hot and cold we publish full frequency for all 80 numbers, draws since each number last appeared, which numbers have come up together, and a breakdown by draw time for anyone wondering whether the 10:01am draw behaves differently from the 6:01pm one. It does not.

    The honest use for all of this

    None of it improves your chance of winning. Twenty numbers are drawn from 80, so every number sits at 25% every single draw regardless of what happened before. What frequency data can legitimately do is tell you which numbers other players favour — birthdays cluster in 1 to 31, and popular combinations get shared more often when they win. Avoiding the crowd does not make you more likely to win; it makes a win worth more if it happens.
