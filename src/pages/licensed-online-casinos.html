Is Online Gambling Legal in NZ? The Law in Plain Terms

For you as a player: yes, and it always has been. New Zealand law has never made it an offence for an individual to gamble on an overseas website. Anyone who tells you that you are breaking the law by playing is mistaken.

For an operator: that is where the law has always done its work. The Gambling Act 2003 is built on prohibition with exceptions — gambling is unlawful in New Zealand unless the Act specifically authorises it. What the Act authorises is a short list, and it is worth knowing precisely because the boundaries explain everything else.

Online casino gambling was not on that list, which created the situation New Zealand has lived with for two decades: offering it from inside New Zealand was unlawful, playing on an offshore site was not an offence, and the offshore operators were beyond the regulator’s practical reach. New Zealand has since legislated to licence online casino gambling under the Department of Internal Affairs, which is what the rest of this page is about.

Online Gambling Laws New Zealand: Who Regulates What

Two bodies, and they do different jobs.

The Department of Internal Affairs is the regulator. It licenses, inspects, investigates and enforces, it administers the online casino licensing regime, and it is the body a player complains to about a licensed operator. If you are looking for an authority on what is permitted, this is it.

The Gambling Commission is a separate independent body. It hears appeals against the Department’s decisions and handles certain casino licensing matters. It is not a complaints service for players, which is a common confusion.

What Does a Casino Licence Actually Do?

Licensing is routinely oversold on comparison sites, so it is worth being precise about what it gives you and what it does not.

What a Licence Gives You

  • A complaints path that does not end at the operator. This is the concrete benefit and the one worth most. On an unlicensed platform the internal complaints team is the last stop, and it works for the party you are complaining about.
  • Enforceable obligations rather than promises. Harm prevention, consumer protection and advertising conduct become licence conditions, which means a regulator can act on a breach.
  • Something the operator can lose. A licence with value attached is a stronger incentive than any voluntary code, because the downside of misbehaving is losing the business rather than losing a badge.
  • Audited games and segregated funds, where required. Most serious regimes require independently tested RNGs and impose rules about holding player money separately from operating money.

What a Licence Does Not Give You

  • Better odds. A licence does not change the house edge by a single basis point. A licensed pokie and an unlicensed one can run the identical game at the identical return — see payout percentages.
  • A guarantee you get paid. It gives you somewhere to escalate. It does not override the operator’s terms, and most withdrawal refusals turn on a term the player actually breached — see fast payout casinos.
  • Protection from your own limits. Licensing conditions require the tools. They cannot require you to use them.
  • Certainty about your deposit if the company fails. Whether player funds are segregated is a question about that operator’s arrangements, and worth asking separately even of a licensed site.

How to Check If a Casino Is Licensed

This is the single highest ratio of protection to effort available to you, and it takes about two minutes.

Step 1: Find the Company, Not the Brand

Scroll to the footer. A legitimate operator names a registered company, a jurisdiction and a licence number. A brand name alone is not enough — many casinos are one of several brands run by a single company, and the company is what holds the licence.

Step 2: Go to the Regulator, Not the Casino

Find the named regulator’s own website and its public licence register. Every credible gambling regulator publishes a searchable list. Search the company name from the footer.

Step 3: Check It Is Current and Covers What You Are Doing

A register entry shows status and scope. A lapsed licence, or one that covers a different product, is not a licence for the thing in front of you.

The test that settles it

If the licence exists only on the casino’s own page — a number in a footer, a seal that is an image rather than a link to a register entry — then it is a graphic, not a licence. Anything can be typed into a footer. A register entry cannot.

The NZ Online Casino Licence: How the Regime Works

New Zealand has legislated to bring online casino gambling inside the regulatory tent for the first time, administered by the Department of Internal Affairs.

Three features of the design are worth understanding, because they are unusual and they shape what the market will look like.

The number of licences is capped. How many online casino licences NZ will issue is settled: no more than fifteen operators. A capped market gives the regulator leverage it has never had over offshore sites, because a scarce licence has value that can be withdrawn.

The right to apply was allocated by auction. Not by assessment alone. Winning at auction is not the same as holding a licence — an operator still has to satisfy the Department on suitability and on its harm-prevention and consumer-protection obligations before one is issued.

Licensed operators will be identifiable two ways. The Department of Internal Affairs publishes a New Zealand online casino licence list — a public register of every DIA licensed online casino — and licensed sites must display a unique registration icon on their platform and in advertising. The register is the authoritative source; the icon is the quick check. Treat an icon with no corresponding register entry as worth nothing, because an image on a web page is trivially copied and a register lookup is not.

Is Gambling Tax Free in New Zealand?

For a recreational player, essentially yes, and it is one of the few genuinely good answers in this area. Gambling winnings are generally not income for tax purposes in New Zealand, and Lotto NZ states prize money is paid in full rather than net of anything. So you do not pay tax on casino winnings in the ordinary case.

Three qualifications worth knowing:

  • What the money earns afterwards is taxable in the ordinary way — interest, dividends, rent.
  • Winning in cryptocurrency raises a separate question. Inland Revenue treats crypto-assets as property, so a disposal can produce taxable income independently of the gambling outcome — see crypto casinos.
  • Professional gambling is a different analysis. Where gambling amounts to a business, the ordinary income tax rules can apply. This is rare and fact-specific.

General information rather than tax advice; Inland Revenue is the right answer for your own circumstances.

What About Advertising — Including This Site?

Online casino advertising is prohibited in New Zealand under the Gambling Act 2003, and the position has been tightened rather than loosened by the new regime: the Department can issue takedown notices and seek penalties of up to NZ$5 million, reaching whoever publishes or arranges to publish an unlawful advertisement rather than only the operator. Sports Betting and Lotto Are Regulated Separately

Betting on sport and racing sits under the Racing Industry Act 2020 alongside the Gambling Act 2003, through TAB NZ, and the online casino regime does not alter it. A law change about online casinos says nothing about a bet on a rugby match, which is a common confusion worth keeping straight.

Lotto NZ’s games sit outside the regime too. Keno, Lotto, Powerball, Bullseye and Instant Kiwi are run by a Crown entity under its own authority, with the surplus going to the Lottery Grants Board rather than to shareholders.
