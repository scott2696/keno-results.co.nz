How it works

Powerball is not a standalone game. You add it to a Lotto line, and a single extra number is drawn from a separate, smaller pool. To take the Powerball jackpot you need all six of your Lotto numbers and the Powerball number.

What it does to your odds

Because the Powerball is drawn independently from its own pool, the chance of taking the top prize is the chance of matching six Lotto numbers multiplied by the chance of matching the Powerball. That makes the jackpot substantially harder to win than Lotto's own first division — which is precisely why the prize is larger.

Powerball also adds its own lower tiers, so a line can win on the Powerball number alongside fewer Lotto matches.

Jackpots

When nobody takes the top prize it rolls over, which is how Powerball reaches the very large jackpots it is known for. A rollover does not change your odds — it changes only the size of the prize you are playing for.

A common misreading

A bigger jackpot does not mean a better chance. The odds of any given line are identical whether the jackpot is at its minimum or at a record high. What changes is the payout, not the probability.

The numbers, exactly

A Lotto line is six numbers chosen from 1 to 40. Powerball is one further number, drawn from its own separate pool of 1 to 10 — rising to 14 from 13 September 2026. Nothing else about the Lotto draw changes when you add Powerball: the same six numbers and the same Bonus Ball are drawn, and the Powerball is drawn alongside them.

The odds, with the working shown

A Lotto line is one combination of six numbers from forty, which is C(40,6) = 3,838,380. Multiply by the number of Powerballs:

Powerball first division odds per line
PowerballsCalculationOdds per line
10 (until 12 Sep)3,838,380 × 101 in 38,383,800
14 (from 16 Sep)3,838,380 × 141 in 53,737,320

Those match the figures Lotto NZ published, and you can check them with a calculator. A ten-line ticket divides the second number by ten, which is where the widely quoted 1 in 5.37 million comes from. The change lengthens the odds by exactly 40% — that is simply 14 divided by 10, and nothing more subtle.

Playing, and where

Powerball is added to a Lotto line rather than bought on its own, through MyLotto or any Lotto NZ retailer. You cannot buy a ticket on this site; we publish results. Draws are Wednesday and Saturday evenings, and the result appears at the top of this page once Lotto NZ publishes it.

What is changing on 13 September 2026

Lotto NZ is taking the Powerball pool from 10 to 14 and adding a Division 8 paying a fixed $12. The starting jackpot rises from $4 million to $5 million and the cap from $50 million to $60 million. More people will win small fixed amounts; the top prize will be struck less often and be larger when it lands. We covered the detail, with the arithmetic checked, in our report on the change.

Can you improve your chances?

Only by buying more lines, and each line costs what it costs. No combination is better than another, past draws tell you nothing about the next one, and no system changes a fixed probability. Anyone selling a Powerball strategy is selling arithmetic you can verify above for nothing.

The one decision that genuinely matters is how much you spend, because that is the only variable you control. If that stops feeling like a choice, there is help.

For current jackpot sizes and prize schedules, check Lotto New Zealand directly — those change, and we would rather point you at the operator than publish a figure that has gone stale.
