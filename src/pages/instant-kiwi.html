Instant Kiwi

Scratch tickets work differently from drawn games — worth understanding before comparing odds.

There is no draw

An Instant Kiwi ticket's outcome is decided when it is printed, not when you scratch it. The whole print run contains a fixed, known number of winning tickets. Scratching reveals a result that already existed.

What the published odds mean

Because the outcome is fixed at print, the advertised odds describe the composition of the entire run — how many winners exist among how many tickets. They are not a per-event probability in the way Keno's odds are.

One practical consequence: as prizes in a run are claimed, the tickets remaining contain fewer winners. Operators publish remaining-prize information for this reason. This is the one respect in which scratch games genuinely differ from drawn games, where past results never affect future odds.

Compared with Keno

Keno's odds are identical for every draw forever, because every draw is independent. Instant Kiwi's effective odds shift over the life of a print run. Neither is beatable, but they are different kinds of game.

Scope

We do not publish Instant Kiwi prize or remaining-ticket data. Lotto New Zealand publish current figures for each game in the range.
