Keno odds

The real probabilities behind every spot count, calculated from the rules of the game rather than rounded for marketing.

Keno draws 20 numbers from a pool of 80. You choose between one and ten numbers — your spots. Everything below follows from those two facts alone.

Taken together, a six-spot ticket matches three or more numbers about 16% of the time, and matches nothing at all about 17% of the time.

How the maths works

These are hypergeometric probabilities — drawing without replacement from a finite pool. For a ticket of s spots, the chance of matching exactly k numbers is:

P(k) = C(20, k) × C(60, s − k) ÷ C(80, s)

where C(n, r) is the number of ways to choose r items from n. Every figure on this page can be verified with that formula.

What the odds cannot tell you

The odds above are fixed. They are identical for every draw, every ticket and every player, and nothing about previous draws changes them.

On systems and strategies

No selection method changes the probabilities in these tables. Choosing numbers that have appeared often, or rarely, or in a pattern, or through a "system", produces exactly the same odds as choosing at random. Anyone claiming otherwise is either mistaken or selling something.

The only choice that genuinely changes your odds is how many spots you play — and more spots means longer odds on the top tier, not shorter.

Prize amounts

We publish odds, not prize values. Payouts depend on your stake and on Lotto NZ's current prize schedule, which the operator can change. For amounts, check with them directly.
