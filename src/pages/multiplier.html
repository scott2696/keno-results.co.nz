The Keno multiplier

Every NZ Keno draw carries a multiplier that scales what a winning ticket pays. Here is how it works, and what we have actually observed across 220 draws.

{subnav}

What it is

Before each draw a multiplier is selected at random. If your ticket wins, the prize for your tier is multiplied by it. A win on a draw carrying ×5 pays five times what the same win would pay on a ×1 draw.

The multiplier applies to the whole draw, not to individual tickets — everyone playing that draw gets the same one. It is published with the result, and we show it on the latest draw and on every row in the archive.

Two things this does not mean

It is not a schedule. These are observed counts over a limited window, not the operator's published probabilities. A larger sample would shift them. Treat the shape as indicative, not exact.

It is not predictable. The multiplier on the next draw is not influenced by recent ones. A run without a ×10 does not make one more likely, in exactly the same way that number frequency does not predict numbers.

Does it change your odds?

No. The multiplier changes what a win pays, never how likely a win is. Your chance of matching numbers is set entirely by how many spots you play — see the odds tables — and is identical on a ×1.5 draw and a ×10 draw.

Because it is drawn before you know the outcome and applies to everyone equally, there is also no way to select for it.
