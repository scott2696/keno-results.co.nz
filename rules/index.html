Read this first

This page explains the rules in plain language. It is not the rules themselves. Keno is operated by Lotto New Zealand under game rules approved by the regulator, and only those documents are binding — on prize claims, disputes, and anything with money attached. Where this page and the official rules differ, the official rules win.

The game

  • Pool: the numbers 1 to 80.
  • Drawn: 20 numbers per draw, selected at random, with no repeats within a draw.
  • Your selection: between 1 and 10 numbers, called spots.
  • Winning: determined by how many of your spots appear in the drawn 20.
  • Prize: set by your spot count, your match count, your stake, and the draw's multiplier.
  • Frequency: four draws daily — see the schedule.

Every draw is independent. Nothing carried over from a previous draw affects the next.

Who runs and regulates it

Keno is operated by Lotto New Zealand (the New Zealand Lotteries Commission), a Crown entity. Its lottery operations sit under the Gambling Act 2003, with oversight from the Department of Internal Affairs. Profits go to the New Zealand Lottery Grants Board for distribution to community causes.

This site is independent of all of the above. We report results; we have no role in operating or regulating the game.

Age restriction

You must be 18 or over to buy a lottery product in New Zealand. This site is intended for the same audience.

Claiming a prize

Prizes are claimed through Lotto NZ, not here. In general terms:

  • Your ticket, or your registered online account, is the proof of entry. Keep the ticket.
  • Smaller prizes can usually be claimed at a retailer; larger ones go through the operator directly and may require identification.
  • Prizes expire. There is a claim window after the draw, and unclaimed prizes are forfeited — check the current period with Lotto NZ rather than assuming.
A match shown here is not a claim

Our ticket checker tells you how many numbers matched. It is an indication for your convenience, not a determination of a win. Confirm any winning ticket with Lotto NZ before acting on it.

Fairness

Draw integrity — the equipment, procedures and auditing that make a draw genuinely random — is the operator's and the regulator's responsibility, and is covered in their published rules. We have no visibility into it and make no claims about it.

What we can say is what we do with the output: every draw we publish is checked against the rules of the game (exactly 20 unique numbers in 1–80) before it is shown, and anything failing is discarded rather than displayed. See our data and validation.

Responsible gambling

Keno carries a fixed house edge, meaning that over enough plays it returns less than it takes in. That is the design, not a flaw, and no way of playing changes it.

  • Decide what you are prepared to lose before you start, and stop there.
  • Treat it as the cost of entertainment, never as a way to make money or recover a loss.
  • With four draws a day, set limits per day rather than per draw.

If gambling is causing harm to you or someone you know, free and confidential help is available from the Gambling Helpline Aotearoa.
