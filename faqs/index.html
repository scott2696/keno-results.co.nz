The game

How does Keno work?

Twenty numbers are drawn at random from a pool of 1 to 80. Before the draw you pick between one and ten numbers — your spots. Your prize depends on how many of your spots appear among the twenty drawn, how much you staked, and the multiplier on that draw.

How many numbers can I pick?

Between one and ten. This is the single most consequential decision on a ticket, because each spot count has its own prize ladder. See the odds tables for all ten.

How often are draws held?

Four times a day, every day. Across our archive the draw times have been consistently 10:01am, 1:01pm, 3:01pm and 6:01pm New Zealand time. The draw schedule has the detail.

What is the multiplier?

A random figure attached to each draw that scales what winning tickets pay. It affects the size of a prize, never the chance of winning one. We have seen it range from ×1.5 to ×10 — see the multiplier page for the observed distribution.

Odds and prizes

What are the odds of matching everything?

It depends entirely on how many spots you play. Matching all 6 on a six-spot ticket is about 1 in 7,753. Matching all 10 on a ten-spot ticket is about 1 in 8.9 million. The full tables cover every spot count and every tier.

Does playing more spots improve my chances?

No — it makes the top tier dramatically harder. What more spots buys you is a longer ladder of smaller prizes, not better odds on the big one. Compare the 4 spot and 10 spot tables.

Why don't you publish prize amounts?

Because they are not ours to publish. Payouts depend on your stake, the draw's multiplier, and Lotto NZ's current prize schedule, which the operator can change. Publishing a figure we could not guarantee would be worse than publishing none. We publish the odds, which are fixed by the rules of the game and verifiable.

Are Keno winnings taxed in New Zealand?

Lottery prizes are generally not taxed as income in New Zealand. Income you later earn on those winnings — interest, dividends, rent — is taxable in the normal way. This is general information, not tax advice; check with Inland Revenue or an accountant for your own situation.

Picking numbers

Are some numbers "due"?

No. Each draw is independent and the draw has no memory. A number that has not appeared in fifty draws is exactly as likely to come up next draw as any other — every number sits at 25% every draw, always.

Then what is the point of your hot and cold page?

It is a record of what has happened, and it is genuinely interesting for one reason: widen the draw window and watch the spread collapse. Over five draws the gap between the most and least drawn number is around 100 percentage points. Over our full archive it is about 15, converging on the 25% baseline. That is randomness becoming visible, and it is the clearest demonstration available that short-run "hot" numbers are noise. See for yourself.

Does a system or strategy help?

No. Every combination of numbers is equally likely. Patterns, birthdays, sequences, wheeling systems and frequency-based picks all produce identical odds. Anyone selling you otherwise is selling you something.

Should I pick unpopular numbers?

It does not change your chance of matching. In games where a prize pool is shared between winners it can affect how much you would split, but the probability of winning is unchanged either way.

This site

Are you Lotto NZ?

No. We are an independent results service, not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated by Lotto New Zealand. You cannot buy a ticket or claim a prize here. They hold the official record for every result.

Where do your results come from?

From Lotto NZ's own published results, read on a schedule shortly after each draw and cached here. Every set of numbers carries a strip naming the source and the exact time we retrieved it. More on our data and validation, and our editorial standards.

Can I trust the numbers on this page?

Every draw is checked twice before you see it — once when we fetch it and again in your browser — against the rules of the game: exactly 20 unique numbers between 1 and 80. Anything failing that is dropped rather than displayed. If we cannot verify a draw, we show nothing rather than a guess.

That said: for anything that matters, confirm with Lotto NZ. We are a convenience, not the official record.

Do you store the numbers I check?

No. The ticket checker runs entirely in your browser. Your numbers are saved to your own device so they are there when you return, and are never transmitted to us or anyone else.

I think a result is wrong. What do I do?

Please tell us — email info@keno-results.co.nz with the draw number and what you expected. We would rather be corrected than be confidently wrong.

Playing sensibly

Can Keno be beaten over time?

No. Keno has a fixed house edge, which means that over enough plays the game returns less than it takes in. That is how it is designed, and no pattern of play changes it. Treat what you spend as the cost of the entertainment rather than an investment.

Where can I get help with gambling?

Free and confidential support is available from the Gambling Helpline Aotearoa. If gambling is causing harm to you or someone you know, contacting them costs nothing.
