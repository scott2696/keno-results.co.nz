Gaming in New Zealand

If Gambling Stops Being Fun

Free, confidential help exists in New Zealand and you do not have to be in crisis to use it. Here is what is available and how to recognise when it is time.

Help now

Gambling Helpline — call 0800 654 655 or text 8006. Free, confidential, and available 24 hours. You can call about your own gambling or someone else’s.

Safer Gambling Aotearoa — safergambling.org.nz for support options and self-help tools.

I run a site about a gambling product, and I think that obliges me to be straight about this rather than bury it in a footer. Every number I publish here — the odds tables, the frequency counts — points the same direction: the game is designed so that, over enough draws, it keeps more than it pays. That is not a scandal, it is how the product works. It only becomes a problem when someone is playing as though it were not true.

The Signs That Actually Matter

Most warning-sign lists are vague enough to be useless. These are the ones I would take seriously:

  • Chasing. Playing to win back what you lost, rather than because you wanted to play. This is the single clearest signal, because it means the loss is now driving the decision.
  • Hiding it. Being vague with people close to you about how much or how often. If it needed hiding, you already know.
  • Borrowed money. Gambling with money meant for something else — rent, bills, someone else’s money.
  • Believing in a system. That a number is due, that a pattern is emerging, that this time is different. I have written at length about why that is not how random draws behave, and the strength of the belief is itself worth noticing.
  • Relief rather than enjoyment. When playing has become something that settles you rather than something you enjoy.

Practical Things That Help

Not a substitute for talking to someone, but these are concrete:

  • Set a limit before you play, not during. A MyLotto account requires you to choose a weekly spending limit, and from 13 September 2026 new accounts must actively choose one rather than defaulting to the maximum. Use it, and set it low enough to be inconvenient.
  • Decide the amount, not the outcome. "I will spend $10" is a plan you can keep. "I will stop when I am up" is not, because the game decides whether that ever happens.
  • Self-exclusion. Operators and venues offer it, and the Gambling Helpline can talk you through how it works.

If It Is Someone Else

The helpline takes calls from family and friends, and you do not need the person’s permission or involvement to ring. You also do not need to have worked out what to say first — that is what they are for.

Frequently Asked Questions About Gambling Harm and Getting Help

Where can I get help with gambling in New Zealand?
The Gambling Helpline on 0800 654 655, or text 8006. It is free, confidential and available 24 hours, and you can call about your own gambling or someone else's. Safer Gambling Aotearoa at safergambling.org.nz has further support options.
Can I call about someone else's gambling?
Yes. The helpline takes calls from family and friends, you do not need the person's permission or involvement, and you do not need to have worked out what to say first.
How do I know if my gambling is a problem?
The clearest single signal is chasing — playing to win back what you lost rather than because you wanted to play, because it means the loss is now driving the decision. Hiding how much you play, using money meant for something else, and believing a number is due are the others worth taking seriously.
