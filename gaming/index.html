The short version

Gambling in New Zealand runs on a simple principle: it is prohibited unless the law specifically allows it. That is the opposite of how most people assume it works. There is no general right to run a gambling operation here and then be regulated — there is a list of what is permitted, and everything outside that list is illegal.

The four things New Zealanders legally play are Lotto NZ’s games (including the Keno results this site publishes), betting through TAB NZ, gaming machines in pubs and clubs, and the six licensed land-based casinos. Online casino gambling has sat outside that structure for years, which is the gap the 2026 Act closes.

How I write these pages

Everything here is sourced from the legislation, the Department of Internal Affairs, or Lotto New Zealand, and linked so you can check it. Where I am uncertain I say so rather than round it off. I am not a lawyer and none of this is legal advice — it is a plain reading of public material, which is what I would want if I were the one looking it up.

Common questions

Is gambling legal in New Zealand?
Some of it. The Gambling Act 2003 works by prohibition with exceptions, so gambling is unlawful unless the Act specifically authorises it. What is permitted is Lotto NZ's games, betting through TAB NZ, gaming machines in pubs and clubs, and the six licensed land-based casinos.
Is online gambling legal in New Zealand?
It is changing. Online casino gambling was never lawfully offered from within New Zealand, and the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 creates a licensing regime for it. From 1 December 2026 only operators that won the right to apply for a licence may serve New Zealand customers. See the law change.
Do I pay tax on gambling winnings in New Zealand?
Lotto NZ states prize money is not taxed, so a prize is paid in full. What the money then earns afterwards — interest, dividends, rent — is taxable in the ordinary way. General information rather than tax advice.
