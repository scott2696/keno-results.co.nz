Powerball gains four balls and a new division on 13 September

Lotto NZ has confirmed the Powerball overhaul launches 13 September: 14 Powerballs instead of 10, a new $12 Division 8, and jackpot odds lengthening from 1 in 38.4 million to 1 in 53.7 million. We checked the arithmetic.

Powerball changes format on Sunday 13 September 2026. Lotto New Zealand confirmed the launch date on 28 August, along with the prize structure that comes with it. The headline is that the game gains four Powerballs and an eighth prize division — and that the top prize gets materially harder to win.

What is changing

The Powerball barrel goes from 10 balls to 14. Everything else about the underlying Lotto draw — six numbers from forty, plus a Bonus Ball — stays as it is.

Around that, Lotto NZ has confirmed:

  • a new Powerball Division 8, paying a fixed $12 for matching two Lotto numbers, the Bonus Ball and the Powerball on one line
  • the starting jackpot rising from $4 million to $5 million
  • the maximum jackpot threshold rising from $50 million to $60 million
  • the fixed Division 7 prize rising from $15 to $20
  • average prizes in Divisions 2 to 6 rising by about 45%
  • no change to the price of a Powerball line

Lotto NZ expects Division 8 to lift the number of Powerball winners in an average week by roughly 30%, from about 47,000 to about 62,000.

The trade, stated plainly

More balls in the barrel means a longer shot at the jackpot. Lotto NZ puts the change at 1 in 38.4 million per line today, moving to 1 in 53.7 million from 16 September.

We checked that arithmetic rather than repeat it. A single Lotto line is one combination of six numbers from forty, which is C(40,6) = 3,838,380. Multiply by the number of Powerballs:

  • 10 Powerballs → 3,838,380 × 10 = 38,383,800
  • 14 Powerballs → 3,838,380 × 14 = 53,737,320

Both round to the figures Lotto NZ published, and their 1-in-5.37-million figure for a ten-line ticket is simply the second number divided by ten. The operator's numbers are correct.

What that arithmetic also shows is the shape of the trade, which is easier to see as percentages. The chance of winning Division 1 on a given line falls by exactly 40% — that is just 14 divided by 10, and nothing more complicated. Against that, the starting jackpot rises 25% and the cap rises 20%. Lotto NZ's own modelling has the jackpot being struck about once every three and a half weeks, against roughly every three weeks now.

So the top prize will be hit less often and be larger when it lands, while far more players win small fixed amounts further down the table. Whether that is an improvement depends entirely on which of those two things you were playing for.

Dates that matter

The last draw under the current 10-Powerball format is a Must Be Won draw on Saturday 12 September, meaning that if nobody takes Powerball Division 1, the jackpot flows down to the next division that has winners. Tickets for the new format go on sale Sunday 13 September, and the first draw under it is Wednesday 16 September.

One thing worth noting

Alongside the format change, Lotto NZ said that from 13 September every new MyLotto account will have to actively choose a weekly spending limit, rather than being assigned the $150 maximum by default. It is a small change to a default, and defaults are usually the part of a system that decides what most people actually end up doing.

Where this came from

Every figure above is from Lotto New Zealand's own media release of 28 August 2026, More Powerballs, more prizes coming 13 September, with the probability calculations worked independently and shown above so you can check them yourself. This is the fifth change to Powerball since the game launched in 2001 with eight Powerballs and a $1 million starting jackpot.
