Keno draws 20 numbers from a pool of 80. You choose between one and ten numbers — your spots. Every figure below follows from those two facts alone, and you can verify any of them with the formula at the foot of this page.

On payouts

We publish odds, not prize values. What each tier pays depends on your stake, the multiplier in play for that draw, and Lotto NZ's current prize schedule, which they can change at any time. For dollar amounts, check with the operator.

How these are calculated

These are hypergeometric probabilities — drawing without replacement from a finite pool. For a ticket of s spots, the chance of matching exactly k numbers is:

P(k) = C(20, k) × C(60, s − k) ÷ C(80, s)

where C(n, r) is the number of ways to choose r items from n. Nothing on this page is estimated.

What the odds cannot tell you

The odds above are fixed. They are identical for every draw, every ticket and every player, and nothing about previous draws changes them.

On systems and strategies

No selection method changes the probabilities in these tables. Choosing numbers that have appeared often, or rarely, or in a pattern, or through a "system", produces exactly the same odds as choosing at random. Anyone claiming otherwise is either mistaken or selling something.

The only choice that genuinely changes your odds is how many spots you play — and more spots means longer odds on the top tier, not shorter. Compare the 10 spot and 4 spot tables above.
